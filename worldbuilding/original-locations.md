# Original timeline — setting and location designs

Status: WORKING DESIGN, 16 September 2026. Rebuild 11 supplies the named places, events and essential geometry. Materials, furnishings and local arrangements added here are new working choices. They describe the physical world without selecting an art style. Read alongside the [setting gazetteer](setting.md), [magic rules](magic.md), [character designs](../characters/original-visuals.md) and [visual continuity notes](../visual-novel/visual-continuity.md).

## Regional ground rules

The active story occupies a temperate river corridor. Working landscape: wooded slopes, quarry cuts, mixed farmland, willow-lined shallows and faster water where rock narrows the channel. Summers allow shirtsleeves and open windows; autumn brings damp decks and river mist; winter requires wool, wet-weather gear and indoor fires. Occasional frost and snow are possible, but perpetual snow is not the northern region's identity. The northern settlements support ordinary agriculture, crafts and households.

The waterway is a route for commerce, military supplies and evacuation. Locks, towpaths, rope, barges and ordinary workers should be visible before the war makes them plot necessities. Boats differ in draft and clearance; a low fishing boat can pass beneath the escape arch where patrol craft cannot. No precise map scale or travel duration is imposed beyond the screenplay. Its five-week convoy includes a journey, not a measurement from which every location's distance can be inferred.

**Orientation constraints:** Rovel's medical landing is downstream from Gray Scar. Harrow has distinct northern and southern approaches. Bellweir's east gate opens onto its causeway over water. The quarry cuts and upper stores are reached through the river network. Bellweir's position relative to every unnamed camp, the full river course and the northern citadel's distance remain unmapped; avoid drawing a definitive continental map from these partial relationships.

## Buildings, utilities and ordinary work

**Working construction:** Rovel favors pale limestone, limewashed walls, tiled roofs and enclosed courtyards. Bellweir mixes brick, timber upper floors and stone quay walls, with red-brown tiles and repaired shutters. Northern administrative and defensive buildings use local gray stone, slate roofing and dark timber; civilian rooms still have plaster, textiles, flowers, toys and firelight. No settlement is uniformly clean, uniformly ruined or uniformly monumental.

Water comes from wells, cisterns and carried household vessels; the large towns also have public water points. Courtyard drains and chamber pots belong to ordinary maintenance rather than a new sanitation subplot. Cooking uses ovens and hearths. Candles and enclosed oil lamps provide portable light. Ward-light is functional magic with a local task, not an assumed electric grid. Mechanical clocks, mills, locks, handcarts, crossbows and printed notices can coexist without assigning the entire world to a real historical century.

Street signs use words plus recognizable trade symbols. Price boards, tally marks and reused paper give commerce texture; exact currency names and exchange rates remain unnecessary. Books are bound working objects that can be copied, annotated, lent and restricted. Notices can be printed or hand-lettered; letter carriage depends on people and transport. No modern screens, ubiquitous mechanical transport, firearms or mass instant communication are added.

**Institutional appearance:** sunburst cloth and brass temple fittings are most concentrated around official human ritual spaces. Human military equipment shares blue-gray coats and practical metal fittings. Northern public buildings and uniforms carry the split white star, on working dark-green cloth. These are institutional marks, not compulsory designs on every civilian possession. Both sides mend equipment and maintain things they value.

## Rovel

### Temple summoning chamber

**Source anchors:** polished stone, glowing circle, arch briefly showing Tessa's store, operated lever, cracks after the passage collapses, crowd and stretcher gallery.

**Working layout:** a rectangular chamber with an arch at its far end. The circle's near edge reaches the arch's threshold, so Tessa can land on it in her first stride onto the stone. At arrival she is just inside that threshold, not posed at the center of the room. Keep the sidewalk, threshold and landing position in a continuous, readable spatial relationship. The earlier several-step gap was an unsupported layout choice that made the scene study imply unexplained movement; it is superseded. The screenplay specifies the transition and her attempted return, but no measured room dimensions or second relocation inside the chamber. The lever is mounted beside the arch, within the scholar's reach. A side gallery leads to infirmary traffic; the route upstairs leaves from the opposite end. Low windows admit dawn light above the spectators. Plain stone around the arch has been repeatedly repaired; no new decipherable ancient inscription supplies the war reveal.

The circle occupies ordinary floor space, with room for Tessa's spilled bag and milk. The audience can see the former street through the arch while it exists. Afterward it is visibly a blocked stone opening with fresh cracks, not a usable doorway into a corridor. Later equipment on the floor belongs to the scholar's work. It does not establish a secretly functioning return mechanism.

### Saint's apartment

A generous room high in the temple complex, with a narrow bed, washstand, wardrobe, table and two wooden chairs. Working details: cream plaster, faded woven rug, heavy curtains and a small unlit hearth. The window looks down over infirmary carts, with the tower Tessa points to visible across the complex. It can be opened far enough for her phone attempt; its height makes it no casual escape route.

The entrance opens onto a guarded hall. Keep a clear place to show the key turning outside and Tessa dragging a chair against the door. The ceremonial dress can lie near the wardrobe without blocking the entrance. Comfortable furnishings and the locking door coexist. No barred dungeon redesign is needed to establish confinement.

### Palace infirmary and royal infirmary corridor

Use one coherent medical complex as a working set family. Long windows illuminate parallel rows of beds with curtains, stools and washable covers. Bowls, folded linen, splints, medicine shelves and patient cards are within actual reach of staff. A covered service court carries carts and deliveries. The window seat used by Tessa and Iven looks toward the convoy yard; the medical chest can stand open nearby.

The corridor has alcoves and benches, with a direct view into Serat's ward. His brother sleeps in a chair at the bed, not in the passage through which Orra and Mara speak. Pale tile behind wash basins and scrubbed timber beds distinguish this from the poorer camp infirmary. Crowding varies by scene; it should not fill every path so completely that ordinary treatment becomes impossible.

### Audience hall, council room and corridor

**Audience hall:** high windows, limestone piers, sunburst banners, a shallow dais with steps and a ceremonial chair. The wounded audience occupies benches and standing spaces. The door is wide enough for Orra and the messenger to remain visible while Senn presents Tessa. Iven can approach the dais from below. The badge, mantle and applause make the ceremony public; no invented coronation is implied.

**Council room:** a smaller working room with a long wooden table, mismatched chairs, map cabinet and writing supplies. One principal door leads to the corridor. Keep Mara, the guard outside and the door's open–closed–open sequence legible. The room's ordinary scale allows Tessa to hold the chair back while everyone else remains close enough to answer her.

**Corridor:** pale stone wall with space beyond the guard's post for Tessa to lean and for Elin to wait. It leads away into occupied offices, rather than ending immediately in a conveniently private alcove. The original book stays inside with Senn when the women leave.

### Reading room, Elin's lodging and relief warehouse

**Reading room:** fixed study tables under tall shelving, candle stands with trays, a staffed issue desk and a secured inner collection. Elin receives books through the archivist; the original is not waiting on an unrestricted shelf. Add felt or cloth pads, page weights, borrowing slips and covered document baskets. The master’s seal comes with Elin's papers, not from a communal stamp tray.

**Elin's lodging:** a modest rented upper room with a narrow bed, wash jug, hooks for a coat, small stove and a worktable under the window. Two chairs can be pulled close over the translations. A short shelf and bundles of copied pages convey limited means and sustained work. It is separate from the archive she later loses access to.

**Relief warehouse:** a requisitioned grain-storage building in the Rovel relief network, its exact district a working choice. High rafters, shuttered ventilation openings, coarse plank partitions and numbered sleeping spaces. Hest kneads at a donated worktable near a managed communal oven; the children's beds occupy the adjacent family space, separated from flour and heat by a low partition. Boots and Orren's cap have places within reach. Keep practical traces of previous storage, along with people making temporary homes.

### River picket

A timber landing adjoining a stone embankment, modest guard shelter, lamp posts and an uphill route toward the infirmary. Litters can move ashore while guards intercept Lucan at the boat-side end. The southern officer cannot plausibly block every medical exit with the two men assigned to stop him. Mooring posts and gangplanks establish the boat's continuing availability when Mara secures his departure.

## The road and Bellweir

### Convoy camp and roadside inn

**Camp:** a wagon pull-off beside a meadow, with tents, tethered animals, a cooking fire and a safe strip for practice. Grain sacks and a stout plank come from convoy supplies. Keep the training area close enough for Iven's applause and the threatened dinner to register. The arriving warden carries the damaged cart board; no crag cat enters this scene.

**Inn:** a low-ceilinged common room, plaster over stone, broad hearth, trestle tables and a narrow stair to guest rooms. The checkerboard sits at the end of a table where Mara can join both players. Keys are ordinary iron keys. Upstairs rooms have interior bolts; the key beside Tessa's cup represents a different practical arrangement from the locked temple apartment.

### Bellweir market, bakery and docks

**Working arrangement:** a sloping market square above the quay. The fountain stands slightly off center so carts have a through-route. Hest's stall and bakery frontage face one side; steps lead toward the lower dock. The east-gate street opens off the opposite side. A narrow alley and its blue workshop doorway sit across from the bakery, separated by the stretch later choked with rubble. These placements are new working geometry supporting the existing action, not a surveyed map.

The fountain has a pale stone basin and a glass heron on a metal turning support. Working glass color is pale blue-green with clear wing edges. A small lever under the stand can stop the rotation. Its physical bearing remains a distinct fitting from the ward-supported foundations below; no new explanation of its power source is required. The basin rim seats two adults and an insistent child.

The bakery is a brick-fronted house with a blue-green shutter, deep counter, oven toward the rear, flour storage and living rooms upstairs. A steep stair has a landing where Orren can later rest on a chair. The family table is large enough to make room for another person. Different dents in the bread boards, mismatched cups, the apprentices' aprons and a repaired bench make it a specific workplace. In the original postwar ending the home has been repaired, not preserved untouched.

The quay below holds rowboats, working barges and floating debris. Bollards, lock gates and a towpath show the town's river function. Orren's leisure boat has a bow bench for the children, positions for two pairs of oars and room for the sketchbook under Tessa's coat. It is not Lucan's launch, a ferry or a cargo barge.

### Hills, east gate and destruction

Wooded hills overlook roofs, docks and the fortress works. Valcair's field table stands below the tree line, with companies moving behind it under hooded lamps. From that position the town can be read as an occupied place, including captured northern barges, without revealing every conversation below.

At the east gate, a broad causeway runs across the water toward open ground. Keep its departure direction stable before and during flight. The bell clock is attached to a civic building near the gate street, not the later Harrow tollhouse clock. Tessa can shelter the crowd from the square back to the gateway; extending protection toward the blue doorway necessarily exposes part of that crowd. The map must preserve that choice.

**States:** first summer has inhabited shops, an intact heron and working homes. The attack breaks ward housings and foundations; black crystal ruptures the bird, buildings physically fall, the gate jams and smoke obscures the blue doorway. Occupation adds the split-star flag. The final spring has rebuilt roofs beside burned walls, repaired paving and a heron still missing one wing. Working choice: its left wing is missing. Inspectors from both banks and a moving grain barge show the changed river arrangement without making the town instantly restored.

## Gray Scar

The ferryhouse stands where two wooded paths meet above an exposed bank. One path carries Mara's families past the dead mule and wrecked wagon; the other brings Lucan's party. The front entrance is the point Mara initially blocks. The timber building has a broad loading exit toward the river, a rear window with a view of the landing gate, and rafters low enough for a catastrophic roof failure to threaten the occupants.

**Locked action geometry:** loading doors → approximately fifty paces of exposed bank → iron gate → sheltered landing beneath a rock overhang → two boats. The overhang protects the landing; it is not above the ferryhouse. The escape route remains broad enough for the litter and surrounding shields. Gate hinges and the wall around it withstand the final adult strike; large openings in neighboring fencing would invalidate the refuge.

Working construction: weathered pine framing, stone footings, tar-darkened door bottoms, rope racks and freight scuffs. The landing gate is a utilitarian iron barrier within rock and masonry, with a bolt operated from inside and space for the ferryman to use his key. At the bank, open sight lines allow crossbow fire and cats to circle beyond the shelter. Boats remain visible as separate evacuation options.

**Crag cats — physical working design:** the source establishes tawny predators, wolf-sized young, a plated adult shoulder and an adult twice a horse's length. Use a low, heavily muscled feline body, broad paws, long claws and overlapping hornlike plates concentrated over shoulder and upper back. Young have smaller plates and climb readily. The adult has a thick balancing tail, rounded ears and pale amber eyes. This is wild animal anatomy, with no harness, faction mark, command signal or familiar bond. The adult can climb the roof and force its collapse; it cannot squeeze bodily through the final gate. Creature behavior follows the visible wounded and slow-moving prey.

## Northern river work and captivity

### Field quarters, wharf and grain wharf

**Field quarters:** a requisitioned stone house with a hearth, drying pegs, campaign table and chairs moved from their domestic uses. The offered chair is near the heat. Valcair waits with the patrol report; Lucan's wet clothes and refusal to sit are visible against this ordinary comfort.

**Household wharf:** timber dock beside stone steps, room for unloading weapons and a mast or staff for the launch's command pennant. The launch has a low cabin, deck space for supplies and a hatch usable as a chart table. It begins serviceable and maintained; loss of military crew and later transfer of ownership are separate events.

**Grain wharf:** larger loading frontage, ramps, sack carts, scales, tally shelters and multiple mooring positions. Loaded barges remain visibly tied up under tarpaulins across the three-day interval. The household supply cart is close enough for Lucan to point to its actual finite contents. A departing crew needs a free route out; the wharf is not a wall of interlocked boats.

### Quarry ramp, camp gate and infirmary

Quarry loads travel from terraced stone cuts to a ramp with a winch and moored work barges. Pale dust coats clothing, barrows and spare stone; the first apprentice can mark the fragment that lands on Lucan's deck. The ramp's edge overlooks the launch closely enough for that throw. The upper quarry has its own guarded landing and is not automatically accessible because Lucan entered another camp.

The camp gate connects a guarded yard to work routes and the infirmary. The governor can see the launch and its food from the gate. A work call carries into the medical building; Orren sees a guard pass the doorway after reaching for his crutches. There are no conveniently open views of every prisoner from outside.

**Infirmary:** former storehouse, rough whitewash, high windows, timber beds, thin blankets, cloth screens and a limited clean preparation table. It is maintained by an overextended healer, not left filthy to suggest medical incompetence. Orren's bedding and old splint still show the conditions of captivity. There is space for Hest to sit close, Tessa to treat the next bed and Lucan to spread a chart. The repeated visits use the same doors, bed positions and window light while dressings and personal possessions change.

### Stores office, upper stores and escape channel

**Stores office:** river-facing issue room with a counter, desks, cargo registers and an inner office visible through its open door. Marren can glance toward the officer; Vask can step out to interrupt. The outer door later closes behind Lucan. Locked cupboards provide ordinary security, not a collection of perfectly labeled villainous secrets.

**Upper stores:** a fortified depot with high windows, guarded entrance and separate loading frontage. Letters and parcels are refused at the entrance. Marren can be seen at a window without being free to speak privately through it. During retreat, crates and clerks move onto a stores barge while two patrol boats prepare to escort it.

The escape route runs through a narrow flooded cut behind the landing, under a low stone arch and around a bend. Working arch purpose: an old quay service opening, not a newly discovered magical tunnel. On the rising water the fishing boat barely clears; its canopy and repaired gunwale scrape, while taller patrol bows stop short. Keep a continuous bank route for the soldiers firing after it. The fishing boat's patch, new leak and Marren's coat against the seam persist until arrival.

### Northern river town

A market street connects moorings, homes and a recruiting hall. Plastered shop fronts, baskets, household pots and an ordinary planted courtyard distinguish civilian life from the military depot. A public notice board holds the named dead beside the false account of southern ward failure. The mother can stand by the recruiting-hall doorway while her son gives his name inside. His father's oversized coat and the remembered family faces carry the loss; a giant triumphal recruitment parade is unnecessary.

## Harrow Ford

### Bridge, approaches and cargo shed

The covered bridge spans a deep, fast channel between stone approaches. “Ford” is the place name; this scene does not offer a safe shallow crossing below. A tollhouse and raised clock sit on the northern approach. A lockable cargo shed stands back from that road. The southern pier leads down toward landings used by the search boats.

Working bridge construction: heavy timber deck, covered truss roof, open side bays and iron reinforcement at the stone ends. The southern brace is identifiable before collapse. Both carts and pedestrians can cross, but household congestion limits rapid reversal. Empty inspected charge housings are visible fittings in the bridge structure; they remain separate from the actual explosives later concealed under the returned-tool wagons.

The shed has wide wagon doors, a side personnel door and room for two loaded wagons. Guards can exclude Marren while engineers work inside. Axles, wheel hubs, numbered seals and layers of returned tools need consistent designs so the purported repair can be compared with what actually changed. No exposed bomb is visible to Tessa from a passing wagon.

### Collapse, bank and relief camp

The wagons fail at midspan; roof and deck fall there. The buckled southern remnant hangs on the iron brace. Orren's stretcher cart is close to that southern stone pier, and Tessa can kneel on the damaged planking while Mara braces behind her on stone. The map must provide the actual height, reach and physical loss of footing described; sanctuary remains magic supporting material, not a substitute for the missing bridge geometry.

The southern bank has a slope, muddy landing and space for receiving recovered people. Lucan arrives from a search boat. Across the broken span his earlier call for ropes does not put him beside Tessa. Downriver, the northern widow and son occupy another part of the same search area, with the sapling among their belongings.

The relief camp is an improvised extension of the exchange's medical arrangements: low tents, canvas screens, stools, washing bowls and a kitchen fire. Iven's body is behind a screen with room for Tessa and Mara outside it. Hest can carry food to northern searchers at the camp edge. This camp is distinct from the first winter's warehouse and the later forward command camp.

## Mill town and campaign country

The occupied mill town follows a short mill channel beside the main river. Working construction: brick workshops, plaster houses, tiled roofs and a stone loading arch large enough to protect halted carts. The barrier seals the main evacuation street. A tower window overlooks that street, with solid masonry beside it for the wardkeeper's cover. A side entrance and narrow stair allow Olan and Tessa to reach him; the back street exists but will not take the loaded carts.

Inside the tower, the top landing has limited room between window and stairwell. The wardkeeper can renew the outside barrier, then drop it and rush the intruders. Keep Olan's retreat toward the stair and Tessa's approach physically possible. Later, a cart is stopped clear of the fight, with space for Mara to sit on its step and Olan to wait with the unhitched horse.

The seasonal campaign needs distinct inhabited places: a bell tower over a small spring market; a summer village whose defenders and residents have withdrawn; river towns receiving southern flags in winter. Reuse regional construction principles, not the identical Bellweir fountain in every village. Weather, vegetation, light through windows, clothing and repaired equipment show seasons without replacing every settlement with snow-covered ruins.

## Forward camp and northern citadel

### Examination tent and command tent

**Examination tent:** canvas sides, a plain table with enough chairs for Marren, his wife, Mara, Elin and Tessa; a closed document case and a separate place for Lucan's sealed personal letter. Escorts wait outside rather than standing over the witness. Diffuse daylight makes the signed papers readable without theatrical interrogation lighting.

**Command tent:** larger table holding Lucan's plan beside Olan's exterior sketch; pinned field reports, a weapons rack and a flap opening onto the assembly area. Distinguish the two sources visually instead of turning them into one authoritative, complete map. The healer receives Iven's bag outside while Tessa prepares.

### Infirmary court and river doors

Gray stone outer walls enclose a more domestic interior: limewashed ward, bed alcoves, small stove, linen shelves and windows facing a covered walkway. The outer gate, west entrance, laundry-side passage and river doors are distinct approaches. The inner court has covered space to which beds can be moved, but it is not immune to attack.

Working layout places the laundry passage along one flank of the outer court, with the river exit behind the medical rooms. Lucan can cross between the gate firing slit and the threatened flank while Serat moves patients deeper inside. Marren's escape through the river doors does not require walking through the assault at the outer gate. The later treatment room for Tessa belongs to this infirmary set family; the exact room can differ from Marren's bed space.

### Valcair's war room and private room

**War room:** broad table, campaign map, winter agreement, supply reports and shaded lamps. Shelves contain rolled maps and ordinary records. The room gives space for Vask to push away and then reclaim the cargo list. Neither ancestral frescoes nor readable historical plaques should accidentally disclose the player investigation's answer to the cast.

**Private room:** smaller hearth, tall-backed chair, wardrobe and a folded clean coat. A plain cup and a well-used pair of house shoes make this an inhabited room. No invented sentimental relic becomes a new explanation for Valcair's war. The distance from door to chair lets Lucan remain by the exit while his father approaches.

### Assault stair, inner landing and throne hall

The assault uses an exterior approach into a narrow turning stair, then an inner landing outside the throne hall. The western passage joins near Mara's bend. Only two attackers can reach her abreast. The upper landing gives Olan room to hold his shield with Elin and incoming wounded behind him; a high window reveals the changing fight at the tower. Do not confuse this route with the infirmary's separate defensive front.

Bronze doors stand physically open while the entrance is blocked by black magical light. Inside are pillars, a stepped dais, throne and a mechanical release behind it. The release controls the entrance ward; it does not switch off Valcair's armor. The last pillar stands close enough to the dais for Tessa's fall, the dropped sword and the spear's reach to connect. Working floor: pale gray stone with narrow dark joints, damaged during the fight. No carpet conceals the point where her hand is pinned.

The post-battle stair retains broken shields, visible repair needs and space for carrying wounded down. Mara's body is at the defended bend. The lower gate is a broader military choke point with armies held on opposite sides and room for medical wagons between them. Opening it for the wounded does not erase the sentries or establish an immediate permanent settlement.

## Earth reference space

Tessa's grocery store appears through the summoning arch: ordinary automatic glass doors, sidewalk, shelf lighting and a fragment of a parking area. The location remains American but unnamed. Do not select a state, real retailer or recognizable skyline as background filler.

Her mother's kitchen is available through Tessa's drawing and any deliberately chosen existing memory reference. Working details: small square table, a pale-green painted chair used by her mother, cream cabinets, crowded refrigerator door, dish towel on an oven handle and a plant beside the sink. The chair gives Tessa a concrete starting point when she redraws the torn page. This description does not add an original-route reunion, a powered phone or a return portal.
