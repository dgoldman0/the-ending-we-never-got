# Neri

## Protected identity and personality

Neri is the outer protagonist: a former ordinary human fan who lived for millions of years, became an Astravus with a vast familiar company and was reincarnated into a High Human body. Her ancient identity is nonbinary; the current local presentation uses she/her. The exact earlier personal name is not established.

She must be brilliant, cunning, playful, whimsical, affectionate and sometimes explosively enthusiastic. Her delight in Tessa is central. Ancient experience can make her perceptive and resourceful without turning ordinary interaction into serene, detached wisdom. Do not replace her with a managerial problem-solver who reports completed preparations.

Her desire to protect and spoil Tessa coexists with wanting Tessa to shine, sharing her life and respecting her decisions. This is Neri's outer-story concern; it is not the definition of Tessa's original struggle.

## Local life — recovered draft choices

Working name: Neri Vale. Parents: Eda and Jory Vale. Home: Mallow Reach. Childhood friend and workshop collaborator: Lark. The older screenplay places her birth nineteen years before the Saint summoning and follows her at six, eleven and nineteen.

Eda teaches sailmaking; Jory works boat fittings. Neri can recall an unfamiliar finishing knot while having difficulty reproducing it with a child's fingers. Model-boat competition with Lark later grows into a winged two-seat skiff. Those are concrete local-life scenes, not proof that an ancient person has forgotten every technical fact.

The user rejected vague passages announcing that she has a family, friendships and 'improbable favors' without actual events. The local childhood needs specific people, conflicts, pleasures and memories. It must not become a long detour that delays the central relationship.

## Recognition and secrecy

Neri does not identify Tessa's world at birth. Names and places are half-familiar; the extraordinary age of her source memories matters. Around the summoning, Tessa's name and ordinary belongings make the connection click. Earlier implementation: a gazette reaches the boatyard four days after the summoning, with a rough woodcut showing the shopping bag.

Partial recognition between the three reincarnators is possible before the full late reveal. The earlier draft uses a remembered tune. Iven and Mara still underestimate her age; she assumes they are contemporaries in experience, not merely in the era of their original fandom. The exact point of partial disclosure should be decided deliberately in the new outline.

Her full history, High Human classification and former Astravus scale remain private until late. Only Tessa, Iven and Mara learn them. The public may witness immense power without receiving the explanation.

## Relationships

**Tessa:** adored fictional heroine becomes a real, self-directing person. Neri gushes, overprepares for meeting her and delights in achievements tiny beside her own abilities. A remembered scene need not correctly reveal Tessa's private preferences; the old seed-pastry scene demonstrates this distinction.

**Pell and the company:** long-established companionship and shared experience; affection, disagreement and independent initiative remain. Neri's relationship is bondkin, not command over disposable summons.

**Iven and Mara:** allies with fresher source knowledge and real professional lives. Neri's experience does not make their contributions redundant. Their death-era misunderstanding can be funny in fragments and moving when resolved.

**Local family and Lark:** genuine attachment and a history built in this life. They see oddities without knowing the cosmic explanation. The family need not become a generic source of approving reactions.

**Lucan and Valcair:** Neri can enjoy Tessa's emerging connection to Lucan and overwhelm the conditions of Valcair's war. She must not turn either into a prop for her approval or solve the ending by mental domination.

## Visual direction

The requested direction was a pretty, youthful, tomboyish young woman around eighteen or nineteen, avoiding an overly masculine face, an exaggeratedly feminine redesign or an apparent mid-twenties age. The working text uses short dark hair, an expressive face and an indigo practical jacket. Exact costume sheets and progression are not finalized.

Rejected visual habits: keeping effectively the same outfit beneath newly added robes; claiming a ground-up redesign after minor changes; distorted anatomy and scale; a creature-like 'pixie' presented as the familiar design; an emotionally cheerful image of a supposedly devastating original aftermath. Do not reconstruct approved art from those rejected images.
