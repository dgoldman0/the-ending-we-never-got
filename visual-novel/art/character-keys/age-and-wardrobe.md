The selected face is the identity anchor across all states. Four years can change grooming, strength, composure and expression without creating a different woman. Avoid generative age jumps, glamour makeovers or permanent exhaustion. Public/formal costume should communicate institutional importance through construction and materials; it need not make her wealthy in every private scene.

## Other cast

- **Ada and Renn:** require genuinely separate child age variants. Ada begins at eight and is roughly eleven–twelve at the endpoint; Renn's working ages are about six and nine. Change height, limbs, face proportions and clothing fit. Keep family resemblance and their individual faces. Do not simply scale up the younger portraits.
- **Apprentices:** mid-teens initially, visibly older in the rebuilt bakery. Preserve the first apprentice's checked neckcloth and the second's distinct oversized charcoal cap.
- **Iven and Mara:** Iven begins at twenty-six and Mara at thirty-two. Apply only the elapsed time before their deaths; no living postwar variants. Wardrobe, repairs and professional bearing carry most of the difference.
- **Elin and Lucan:** Elin begins at twenty-two; Lucan reads as mid-twenties. Modest maturation over the story, with explicit field/injury variants. Neither becomes middle-aged during these events.
- **Valcair, Senn, Orra, Vask and recurring adults:** preserve the developed apparent-age ranges. Ordinary year-to-year changes are subtle; their scene states and costume changes matter more than additional wrinkles.
- **Families after the war:** clean work clothes, healed/recovering states and continued work. Do not copy captivity dirt or fresh wounds into every later key.

Exact birthdays and intermediate ages are not established. Label by screenplay period where a precise age cannot be inferred. Apparent-age references are not new claims about northern biology or lifespans.
