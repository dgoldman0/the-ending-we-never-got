## Construction

One worn blue-gray woven coat closes with small dull brass buttons to its own modest upright collar. The collar is sewn directly into the coat body; the narrow pale botanical trim is its edge binding, not a second collar. The same cloth continues down the chest. Narrow worn ivory and dull-gold botanical borders follow the collar, front, cuffs and skirt hem. Repairs and faded seams record use without adding separate shoulder pieces, a tunic front, scarf or cape.

The divided skirt opens below the waist over charcoal trousers. Scuffed calf boots enclose their ends. Both hands wear short brown leather gloves, with no brace or injury. A worn leather belt supports the sheathed sword at her anatomical left hip through a visible hanger. This is a costume stance, not proof of a complete sword draw, climbing motion or kneeling clearance.

The small brass twelve-ray temple badge stays on her anatomical left upper chest. The narrow upright collar distinguishes this coat from the broad rolled winter collar and the field garment's broad pale botanical yoke. It remains a human-side blue-gray garment, with no northern green or split-star emblem.
