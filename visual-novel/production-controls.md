# Visual judgment and production after the Rovel failure

**Active correction, 17 September 2026.** The user rejected 0.4.0-dev, archived at `1bb6d76`. Read the [illustrated failure analysis](reviews/2026-09-17-rovel-rejection/README.md) before another visual pass. The [experience brief](presentation-redesign.md) still defines the game. This document changes how a visual direction is established and judged; it does not start another production pass.

## The failure was in judgment

The previous session inspected the images and accepted obvious visual faults. It already had requirements for beauty, good crops, complete compositions and manual review. The problem was the model's judgment, not the absence of instructions to look. Favorable review prose and technical reports then gave that weak judgment an appearance of authority.

**Documentation cannot guarantee better visual judgment.** Nor does another model using the same assumptions provide independent artistic validation. These controls make the standard concrete, require demonstrated visual discrimination and keep an unproven judgment from directing hours of production. If a model cannot recognize the illustrated defects below, it has not demonstrated fitness to approve the visual direction. Repeating a review ritual does not qualify it.

The current request authorizes the failed-attempt commit and documentation/control update. It does not authorize new scene art, a UI redesign or portrait repairs. A diagnostic question calls for an answer; do not infer permission to edit from it. Later instructions can authorize production, and routine work already authorized needs no repeated permission request.

## Establish the standard with actual pictures

Open the [rejected examples](reviews/2026-09-17-rovel-rejection/README.md#visual-failures-the-review-should-have-rejected). They remain unacceptable even though the files load, the text fits and some individual faces are detailed. Keep them available as counterexamples; never use them as likeness, style or generation references.

There is currently **no user-approved runtime interface**. The selected character/scene references establish identity, painted materials and atmosphere; they do not approve these gilt rectangles, detached heads or utility bars. Do not invent approval for an assistant's preferred design. A phrase such as “painfully beautiful” is not a concrete visual target by itself.

Before producing a new frame or expression sheet, make two or three materially different **complete screen compositions** with the same real scene and dialogue. Include portraits and controls at playing size. Compare hierarchy, scene visibility, balance, portrait treatment and reading path. Changing the border texture on the same layout is not a distinct solution. Explain the visual reason for preferring one; ease of stretching or reuse is not that reason.

Use authoritative likeness/material references for their stated purposes. If additional examples are needed to establish interface quality, distinguish comparative references from approved project direction. Evaluate the relevant craft without copying another game's identity. Never treat the rejected runtime as the highest quality benchmark simply because it is local.

## Look before reading the explanation

Open the final rendered screen with text and controls visible at ordinary playing size. Form the initial visual assessment **before reading generation prompts, implementation notes, checklist results or a prior positive review**. Those describe intent and can bias a reviewer toward seeing successful compliance. A reviewer who created the image is not blind to that history; do not call that an independent blind review.

Describe the actual picture. Here, useful observations are “two heads end at their necks against a decorative border,” “one line sits over a large empty panel,” and “the treatment instruction covers the lower arm area.” “Compact portraits are present,” “all frame edges exist” and “the dimensions fit” do not establish a professionally composed result.

Judge the whole arrangement first. If it looks awkward, crude, cluttered or disconnected, identify the dominant cause and revise the composition before polishing every component. A clean mask can still be an ugly crop; individually attractive ingredients can still make an ugly screen.

| What the eye should notice | Judgment the reviewer must make |
| --- | --- |
| A large, nearly empty panel under a short reply | Does the space help the composition? Short and long lines both need deliberate treatment. Longest-line capacity does not justify the short-line result |
| Ornament intersects hair/neck; the lower neck contour hangs free | Does the portrait belong to the surface? Use an intentional crop, bust, inset or other boundary; removing the background alone does not finish a portrait |
| Uneven apparent head sizes and lower cuts despite equal square files | Compare eye level, headroom, gaze room, face scale and lower boundary across characters and expressions |
| A tiny listener at the far end of a large empty box | Can the reader meaningfully relate that reaction to the line? Presence alone does not establish useful placement |
| The UI covers the scene's meaningful action | Recompose art and text together. Preserve key faces, hands, contact, props and routes during reading; a hide-UI shortcut does not repair the default composition |
| Awkward duplicated faces, or protagonists absent from meaningful staging | Direct the relationship between scene and compact acting for this beat; no universal mode or actor-removal rule can decide it |
| An elaborate frame and generic utility strip feel unrelated | Judge type, material, spacing, grouping, focus and control prominence as one visual family |

These judgments must be demonstrated with pictures, not by repeating the desired wording. The [illustrated failures](reviews/2026-09-17-rovel-rejection/README.md#visual-failures-the-review-should-have-rejected) do not become good because someone can explain why every component exists.

## Compose portraits before automating crops

Decide head versus bust, visible clothing, crop boundary, camera angle, eye line, scale and space around the face before requesting a sheet. Compact frames remain required where useful; head-only cutouts are not prescribed. If clothing is visible, use the correct wardrobe rather than cutting away the body to avoid continuity work.

Compare identity to selected references and written designs, then compare the finished portrait with others in the interface. Inspect hair, ears, chin, neck, shoulders where present, and the frame relationship. A recognizable face can still be an unsuitable dialogue portrait.

Open every selected portrait **composited on its actual UI surface**, at speaker and listener sizes, in Intense and Softened. Inspect the final export after every crop/mask/grade change. A raw PNG viewer can display RGB beneath transparent alpha; confirm the visible composite before asserting a background defect. Review the whole portrait and native detail. A contact sheet is an index, not sufficient inspection.

Automate only a treatment demonstrated to suit the actual pose. Do not apply one fixed contour to differently posed heads and infer success from clean exports. Supporting speakers need the same care as leads; brief appearance does not make a bad portrait acceptable.

## Demonstrate one finished example before expanding

The next production delivery, when requested, is the compared layouts and **one short, polished connected exchange**. Include actual speaker changes, a meaningful listener reaction, an action/narration beat and the blend with detailed scene art. Use real short/long dialogue and relevant lighting/text-size conditions. Include discovery/return where it belongs to the passage; do not force an unrelated checklist scene into the source.

Read the full source interaction first. Keep the experiment separate from the shared runtime so one screen edit does not silently apply an unproven design to 95 pages. Produce only the cast/components needed for the example. S001–S005 remains the subsequent complete milestone, not the size of the first demonstration. The complete original route remains in scope.

Deliver the real visual result and candid assessment before hours of expansion. **The producing model's favorable self-review cannot alone establish the new direction after these failures.** Use the user's response to the concrete delivery, or a visual reviewer explicitly designated by the user, to calibrate direction before rollout. Do not invent approval or treat silence as agreement. The lead must remove obvious defects first; this checkpoint is not an invitation to make the user perform basic cropping and layout QA.

Once direction is established, keep those selected complete-screen examples beside subsequent interface work. Character/scene references retain their separate authority. Compare later screens against that standard; a different cast, lighting register, text length or scene mode can expose new defects. Template reuse does not establish consistent quality.

## Make criticism change the work

State serious visible defects before accomplishments. Tie each to an image region or transition and identify the required change. Do not defend a failed image with its prompt, tests or time invested. If a revision retains the dominant problem, reconsider the composition instead of adding another local repair or decorative layer.

The lead owns the integrated judgment. Component reviews do not add up to a whole-screen pass. If delegation is explicitly requested, reviewers should make their own observations from final images before reading the maker's positive review; shared assumptions still limit independence. Do not start a team merely because the prior attempt used one.

Play the finished passage at a plausible reading pace. Note where attention is pulled away, acting becomes repetitive and navigation interrupts reading. Automated traversal does not experience pacing; an assistant's timing estimate is not measured human reading time. Stills support composition review but cannot clear connected rhythm.

Actual defects are **failed**. Missing evidence or uncertain quality is **unreviewed**. Neither supports expansion. A favorable verdict must explain the visible improvement using final evidence and state its limits. If the reviewer cannot distinguish an improvement from the rejected examples, leave direction unproven and deliver the limited result honestly. More generation cannot repair an inability to judge the output.

## Supporting checks and handoff

Source fidelity, state, geometry, accessibility, masks, exports and packaging remain engineering work. Their results cannot judge beauty and must not enforce rejected design decisions. The Rovel checker no longer requires every spoken line to be compact, every stage actor to disappear, every portrait to be head-only, or the old layout-data architecture.

There is no new build gate, automatic visual score or production-state JSON. Scripts do not repair model perception. The spending control is the small early delivery and demonstrated visual direction. The artistic control is concrete comparison and judgments borne out by the pictures and external feedback. Neither guarantees success; they expose failure earlier and reduce repeated expansion of weak work.

Keep current status consistent in instructions, the experience brief, route ledger and runtime/QA guides. Preserve historical reviews under explicit superseded labels. Commit useful progress accurately; commits do not confer artistic approval. End this task with the failed archive and documentation/control update, not another unrequested production attempt.
