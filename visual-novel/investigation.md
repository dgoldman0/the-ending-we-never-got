## Accepted interaction contract

The player controls attention and connections. There is no player body in the fiction: no first-person corridor traversal, opening doors, questioning people, inventory, travel or new actions attributed to the cast. Movement between views is editorial reframing—a close view, a juxtaposition, a change of perspective or an authored historical cutaway. A cutaway gives the reader access to a scene; it does not make the reader a character present there.

The interaction:

1. **Look closer:** pause at a meaningful illustrated detail and inspect it at a useful scale. The image, object or action must carry something the player can notice; an empty hotspot that opens an explanatory essay is insufficient.
2. **Bring details together:** follow a visible relationship to an already encountered moment or another available view. Keep the originating detail available so the player can actually compare them.
3. **Follow the connection:** reveal additional authored material or a further perspective that deepens understanding. The connection must make sense from what was inspected; reward curiosity with substance rather than a completion badge.
4. **Threads:** revisit available details and the connections followed between them. The organization should show relationships and retain the path back, not become another case checklist or a spoiler-filled encyclopedia.
5. **Return:** resume the exact story beat. Reading time does not advance the story clock, and discovery does not change the original outcome.

No multiple-choice comprehension tests, correct/incorrect grading, compulsory clue quotas or quiz-completion gates. No consumable clues, countdown, survival score or failure ending. Provide visible, accessible inspection targets with keyboard/list equivalents; avoid precise pixel hunting. Previously available material can be revisited without replaying chapters. Story progress can protect future scenes from spoilers; reading every item is not a homework requirement.

Character-knowledge restrictions belong in authoring and state management. Convey the player's reader position through the interaction's form; do not display disclaimers about what characters know, production limitations or unwritten canon. Tessa, original Iven, original Mara, Elin and Lucan retain exactly the knowledge their scenes establish.

## The first Saint and the start of the war

This should be a reconstructable history with a reliable answer. The player must eventually understand what humans initiated and how the first Saint summoning belonged to it. Merely finding that both sides suffered, that official history is incomplete, or that Valcair has a grievance would not satisfy the requested reveal. Authored historical scenes, older images and source material are possible forms; the record table below proposes information functions without mandating a document-collection interface.

The following are **proposed exhibit functions**, not already discovered screenplay props or approved historical documents. Develop their actual wording and events together before production.

| Exhibit | Information it must supply | Player operation / intended finding |
| --- | --- | --- |
| H01 — later human account of the first Saint | The later version of the founding event and its claimed reason for summoning | Identify its claims without treating a polished institutional account as proof. Its particular author and date are still open. |
| H02 — contemporary human summoning record | Human authorization of the first Saint summoning and enough of its original purpose to examine the initiating act | Compare the contemporary purpose with H01. Do not infer the summoned person's willing participation from an institution's decision. |
| H03 — record of the conflict's opening sequence | The relationship between the first summoning, the initiating human action and the northern response | Place events in relative order. The specific first operation and harm need a deliberate history-writing decision; do not fill them with an invented massacre, named treaty or new power just to complete a puzzle. |
| H04 — independent corroboration | A second perspective that supports the sequence and its effects without relying solely on Valcair's account | Cross-check events and omissions. Exact witness, medium and chain of preservation remain open; two copies of one account do not count as independent support. |

Proposed discovery order: material becomes available across R02–R05's story spans, with earlier views remaining accessible. The thread must support the explicit discovery: **humans initiated the ancient conflict through the first Saint summoning; the later account obscured or recast that origin.** Author the initiating sequence and meaningful connections before implementing the reveal. Do not simply display that sentence in a card and test recall. The player may infer it earlier; the interface should not deny a supported connection merely to preserve a scheduled reveal.

Write enough surrounding detail for each exhibit to answer a real question: who is making the claim, which event it describes, what its author could have known, how its time is established and what corroborates it. Resolve these particulars before implementing the history round. Do not use decorative illegible text as an apparent evidence system.

Historical views belong to the reader's access. They do not become additions to Elin's summoning book or discoveries made by the cast. Presentation must carry that convention without the rejected character-knowledge disclaimer. Do not introduce forged-looking documents with undeveloped authors, dates or provenance merely to give the player something to click.

Keep the conclusion's limits equally clear: historical human aggression does not excuse Harrow, remove Vask's agency, prove every human ruler guilty, or establish the first Saint's own motives. Do not assume Valcair witnessed every ancient event or that his present atrocities were inevitable.

## Additional knowledge beyond the central reveal

The mode can deepen the world through connected questions rather than a pile of unrelated entries:

- **Saint work:** the distinction between cleansing a curse, treating a wound and replacing missing tissue; what the institution asks of a summoned person. Source demonstrations provide the first evidence.
- **The river:** how locks, boats, food and prisoner labor constrain rescue and diplomacy. Use the already established local relationships; do not invent a full continental map or arbitrary travel distances.
- **Institutional memory:** how the first summoning is represented in later accounts, and which original evidence complicates that account. This is new history work, with the scope above.
- **Harrow:** how a real inspection was bypassed, how selective recall protected one person, and how the public account fed recruitment. The investigation can give these connections attention without extending the collapse or forcing Tessa to revisit her grief for clues.

Do not invent hidden confessions in Iven's portrait, read Lucan's unwritten personal letter, or turn the damaged hand into a medical deduction challenge.

## Knowledge and progression rules for later implementation

| State | What advances it | What it may change |
| --- | --- | --- |
| Story progress | Completing the authored scene sequence | Available scenes, current time, visible people and source-established character knowledge |
| Inspected details | The player examines an available detail view | Which details can be revisited and brought alongside each other |
| Followed connections | The player follows an authored relationship | Available branches of Threads and additional discovery material |
| Ancient-origin discovery | Following the authored historical connections | Explicit player knowledge only; no dialogue, romance, battle or survival flag |
| Original ending reached | The completed Bellweir epilogue | Access to the final question, irrespective of investigation progress |

Do not allow chapter selection, save loading or Threads to expose future story details unintentionally. Save inspected details, followed connections and the return point together; loading an earlier save must not retain discoveries from a later playthrough. No access to “Do you wish to save Tessa?” is gated behind this optional material.
