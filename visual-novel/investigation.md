# Player investigation — first design sketch

Status: WORKING GAMEPLAY PROPOSAL. The user wants enough investigation to discover additional information, including **humans initiating the ancient war through the first Saint summoning**. That connection is now a user-established historical anchor. Tessa's summoning is a later event. The exact first Saint, dates, commissioning individuals, first operation and Valcair's firsthand involvement remain to be developed.

The investigator is the **player**. Tessa, original Iven, original Mara, Elin and Lucan do not acquire the ancient truth through these screens. Read with the [route outline](outline.md), [pacing plan](pacing.md), [knowledge boundaries](../story/knowledge-and-reveals.md) and [war history](../worldbuilding/war-and-history.md).

## A mode with things to do

Propose a separate **Investigation** screen at five quiet chapter boundaries, also accessible later for unlocked material. It is an interface for the reader, outside character inventories and the story clock. It can show details from scenes already witnessed and present new historical material directly to the player. Entering it does not mean Tessa walks to an archive, spends a day researching or carries its documents into the next scene.

Each round has a concrete question, several evidence views, at least one comparison and a conclusion the player can establish. The historical thread builds across rounds. This should have enough substance for sustained attention, rather than a glossary button containing the answer.

Proposed loop:

1. Choose a question from the currently available round.
2. Open a place/object/record view. Inspect marked details or use an equivalent list; every necessary detail is discoverable without precise pixel hunting.
3. Compare two views, put events in relative order, or distinguish an observation from a claim. These are reading and reasoning actions, not new actions for the cast.
4. Assemble a conclusion with its supporting material. The interface distinguishes established findings, claims made by a source and questions still open.
5. Continue examining, request a guided explanation, or return to the story at the exact saved point.

No consumable clues, move limit or failure ending. An unsupported inference brings the relevant material back into view rather than scoring the player as immoral or careless. All essential story scenes play even if the player never opens this mode. Choosing to investigate earns explicit additional knowledge; it does not improve a hidden survival statistic.

The opening rounds can be shorter; later historical work can support a longer session or several returns. A **provisional reading target** is roughly 5–8 minutes for R01 and 10–15 minutes for each later round: about 45–70 optional minutes overall. These are planning allowances, not measured playtime. Test R01 before committing to a word count or increasing the number of exhibits.

## The first Saint and the start of the war

This should be a reconstructable history with a reliable answer. The player must eventually be able to state what humans initiated and how the first Saint summoning belonged to it. Merely finding that both sides suffered, that official history is incomplete, or that Valcair has a grievance would not satisfy the requested reveal.

The following are **proposed exhibit functions**, not already discovered screenplay props or approved historical documents. Develop their actual wording and events together before production.

| Exhibit | Information it must supply | Player operation / intended finding |
| --- | --- | --- |
| H01 — later human account of the first Saint | The later version of the founding event and its claimed reason for summoning | Identify its claims without treating a polished institutional account as proof. Its particular author and date are still open. |
| H02 — contemporary human summoning record | Human authorization of the first Saint summoning and enough of its original purpose to examine the initiating act | Compare the contemporary purpose with H01. Do not infer the summoned person's willing participation from an institution's decision. |
| H03 — record of the conflict's opening sequence | The relationship between the first summoning, the initiating human action and the northern response | Place events in relative order. The specific first operation and harm need a deliberate history-writing decision; do not fill them with an invented massacre, named treaty or new power just to complete a puzzle. |
| H04 — independent corroboration | A second perspective that supports the sequence and its effects without relying solely on Valcair's account | Cross-check events and omissions. Exact witness, medium and chain of preservation remain open; two copies of one account do not count as independent support. |

Proposed discovery order: R02 introduces H01 and the existence of the earlier record; R03 opens H02; R04 opens H03; R05 supplies H04's independent corroboration and the complete comparison. Earlier material remains accessible so a skipped round does not require replaying chapters. Once the evidence has been examined, the conclusion is stated clearly in the player's findings: **humans initiated the ancient conflict through the first Saint summoning; the later account obscured or recast that origin.** The completed inquiry should explain the authored chain, not merely award that sentence as a prize. The player may infer it earlier; the interface should not deny a supported conclusion merely to preserve a scheduled reveal.

Write enough surrounding detail for each exhibit to answer a real question: who is making the claim, which event it describes, what its author could have known, how its time is established and what corroborates it. Resolve these particulars before implementing the history round. Do not use decorative illegible text as an apparent evidence system.

These records appear directly in the player's investigation interface. They are not additions to the summoning book Elin obtains, readable wall inscriptions beside the cast, or documents Tessa secretly finds. Their existence does not imply that every contemporary resident has read them. The interface's access convention should be introduced plainly at R01: its collected views belong to the reader; the story's people know only what their scenes establish.

Keep the conclusion's limits equally clear: historical human aggression does not excuse Harrow, remove Vask's agency, prove every human ruler guilty, or establish the first Saint's own motives. Do not assume Valcair witnessed every ancient event or that his present atrocities were inevitable.

## Additional knowledge beyond the central reveal

The mode can deepen the world through connected questions rather than a pile of unrelated entries:

- **Saint work:** the distinction between cleansing a curse, treating a wound and replacing missing tissue; what the institution asks of a summoned person. Source demonstrations provide the first evidence.
- **The river:** how locks, boats, food and prisoner labor constrain rescue and diplomacy. Use the already established local relationships; do not invent a full continental map or arbitrary travel distances.
- **Institutional memory:** how the first summoning is represented in later accounts, and which original evidence complicates that account. This is new history work, with the scope above.
- **Harrow:** how a real inspection was bypassed, how selective recall protected one person, and how the public account fed recruitment. The investigation can give these connections attention without extending the collapse or forcing Tessa to revisit her grief for clues.

Do not invent hidden confessions in Iven's portrait, read Lucan's unwritten personal letter, or turn the damaged hand into a medical deduction challenge.

## Knowledge and progression rules for later implementation

| State | What advances it | What it may change |
| --- | --- | --- |
| Story progress | Completing the authored scene sequence | Available scenes, current time, visible people and source-established character knowledge |
| Viewed evidence | The player opens an available evidence view | Which views can be compared or revisited |
| Established player findings | Supported comparison or requested guided explanation | The player's findings text and further investigation material |
| Ancient-origin discovery | Completion of the authored historical chain | Explicit player knowledge only; no dialogue, romance, battle or survival flag |
| Original ending reached | The completed Bellweir epilogue | Access to the final question, irrespective of investigation progress |

Do not allow chapter selection, save loading or the investigation index to expose unencountered story evidence unintentionally. The mode must return to the same story beat and preserve the distinction between reading an exhibit and performing an in-world action. No final answer to “Do you wish to save Tessa?” is gated behind collecting these records.
